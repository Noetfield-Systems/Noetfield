# Vision film — "Noetfield in full production"

The second class of film on the Evidence Lab. This one is **not** a recording
and must never be mistaken for one.

`SCRIPT.md` is the demo film: one real run, every beat verified against
production, nothing claimed that a stranger could not reproduce. This is its
opposite number — the product at full scale, made rather than captured — and it
earns its place only because it is labelled as such everywhere it appears.

The player carries `CONCEPT — NOT A RECORDING` burned into the frame, so a
screenshot, an embed or a crop still says what it is. That label is what buys
the ambition below. Unlabelled, every beat here would have to shrink to what
exists today and it would still be the first thing a diligence reader attacks.

## The rule for this film

Each beat states the intended product, and then — in the same breath, in the
notes, not the voiceover — what is true today. The film may show the intention.
The page may not imply it has arrived.

If a beat cannot name its today-state honestly, cut the beat.

## Messaging locked

Same as the demo film, and for the same reason:

- **Cost:** "under a quarter per build". Never the raw fraction of a cent.
- **Phase language:** generation is live; hosted auto-running is Enterprise;
  TrustField is the regulated vertical. The film may *show* Phase 2. The
  voiceover must not say it ships today.
- **Never** "fully autonomous", "no human needed", "replaces your team". The
  entire product argument is that a human keeps the promotion decision.

## The beats

Target 60–75 seconds. Shorter than the demo film on purpose: this one carries
no evidence, so it has less right to a viewer's time.

### 0–10s — the room

> "This is what a company looks like when the work arrives finished."

Wide shot of the workspace at scale: many jobs in flight, each with its own
state, none of them being assembled by hand. One operator, watching.

*Today:* founder-operated alpha, one operator, a handful of concurrent jobs.
The scale is the intention. The shape is real.

### 10–24s — the order

> "Someone asks for what they need, in a sentence. Not a ticket. Not a spec."

A plain request goes in. The plan comes back naming everything it will produce —
the page, the automation, the checks it will have to pass.

*Today:* **this is real and shipped.** Filmed for the demo film on production.
This beat is the one place the vision film and the recording overlap.

### 24–40s — the factory

> "It is bounded before it runs. A budget, a task graph, and a verifier that is
> not the thing being verified."

Show the work fanning out and coming back. Authority stated up front, spend
ceiling visible, the check running separately from the build.

*Today:* real — bounded execution, separate verification, budget ceilings, all
enforced. What is intended is the **scale**: many of these at once, supervised
rather than watched.

### 40–52s — the refusal

> "It still refuses to invent a fact it was never given. At any scale, that
> never becomes optional."

The stop, the stated reason, the question back to the client.

*Today:* **verified end to end** and the strongest beat in the demo film. It
belongs here too — the vision is not that the machine gets bolder, it is that
it stays this careful while doing far more.

### 52–65s — the evidence

> "Every accepted outcome leaves a receipt. Not a log. A receipt someone else
> can open."

The wall of completed work, each item with its own evidence, each openable.

*Today:* receipts are real and published. Intended: the volume, and the client
seeing their own wall rather than a founder publishing one.

### 65–75s — the close

> "Agent execution assurance. The work arrives planned, checked and evidenced —
> and a person still decides what ships."

Hold on the promotion step. A human hand, one decision.

*Today:* human promotion is real and enforced. Ending here is not a hedge — it
is the product. Anything that ends on "and it runs itself" sells the opposite
of what this company is for.

## What this film must not show

- **Hosted auto-running as a present-tense fact.** A form submission producing a
  live email is Phase 2. It may appear as intention; it may not appear as
  something a viewer could go and do today. Same boundary the demo film's close
  had to respect.
- **Named third-party customers**, real or implied. There are none yet, and a
  fabricated logo wall on the evidence page would end the argument.
- **Invented numbers on screen.** Dashboards in frame should carry no figures
  that are not either real or obviously illustrative. A rendered "$2.4M ARR" in
  a screenshot is a fabricated record, label or no label.
- **A cost figure per run**, unless it is the measured one and stated as one
  recorded build.

## Production notes

- Captions only, no voiceover-over-music, matching the demo film. ElevenLabs
  narration is fine and matches the enterprise register — keep it flat and
  analytical, never triumphant.
- Rendered or composited footage is expected here; that is what makes it a
  concept. There is no obligation to film real screens, and no permission to
  present rendered screens as real ones.
- Keep it visually distinct from the demo film. If the two are cut in the same
  house style, the label on the frame is doing all the work of separating them,
  and labels get cropped.

## Where it goes

The reserved slot in the Product vision section of `/proof/lab/`, id
`vision-001-full-production`. Publishing is one field on that slot plus the
matching entry in `/proof/lab.json`; the maintenance block in that file has the
steps.

A concept film never receives a run receipt. There is no run behind it, and
inventing one would defeat the page it sits on.
